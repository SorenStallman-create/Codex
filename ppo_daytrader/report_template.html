Does a bigger network help?

The capacity test. The deep network reads the last 16 hours of raw bars (return, upper and lower wicks, gap, volume, range) through a dilated convolution, plus the same engineered features, plus VIX context from the Equibles connector. The swing rows use the compact network on 4-hour bars with positions held for up to 30 days. Same costs, folds and checkpoint rule. All test days pooled; swing figures are converted to a per-day rate.

Round 2: attacking the cost gap

Every arm below was scored on the six 183-day validation windows only, and the winner was frozen before any of its test years were looked at. "Limit" arms rest an order at the bar's open that fills only if price trades 5 bp through it, paying maker fees (0.02% perp, 0.10% spot), with forced session exits as market orders. "Linear" arms trade a ridge forecast only when it clears the fee. "Season" picks one fixed window of UTC hours from the training data.

Is there anything to learn?

A first-principles ceiling check. If a plain linear forecast of the next bar can't rank returns out of sample, a reinforcement learner on the same inputs has little to find either. Rank IC is the Spearman correlation between the forecast and the realized 15-minute return.
